<b>Engineering Leadership:</b> I was promoted to Principal Engineer, which is a very big name for a bear. I became the unofficial tech lead of one of the product teams. I led cross-functional teams, and I helped set the technical vision for secure web applications. Besides my daily work in the front-end and back-end codebases, I looked after many other things: the engineering culture, the technical stack decisions, excellence across teams, the technical parts of hiring, mentoring, and more.

<b>AI-Powered Solutions:</b> I worked closely with AI researchers, ML specialists, and ETL data engineers. Together we made applications for people to use, with complex AI inside. I joined advanced audio processing to the applications, for transcription, denoising, and translation in many languages. Then I added image processing for humanitarian crisis response, which means helping people when things go very wrong.

<b>Frontend Architecture:</b> I planned important applications that must not fall over. I used React, TypeScript, and Next.js, with MUI components, and I kept my eye on Core Web Vitals. I used React GL to make complex geospatial screens, which are maps. They gave humanitarian early warning systems interactive maps.

<b>Performance Optimization:</b> Slow things are hard for people who have very little. I used standard front-end profiling tools to find where things got slow. Our users do not have much to work with. I used code-splitting, lazy loading, and SSR/SSG techniques, so bundles got smaller and loading got quicker. I also made React draw things more gently, for a great UX.

<b>Progressive Web Applications:</b> Some of our users in Syria have a wobbly internet, and phones that cost little. I thought about that for a long while. Then I built offline-first PWAs, with local-first, optimistic UI and RTL support. These applications are very important to them.

<b>Modern Development Stack:</b> I moved many repositories to newer JavaScript development tools, like pnpm, Vite, and Vitest. I also replaced a couple of GraphQL-based applications with Next.js. Next.js does server-side rendering, and it makes API links between client and server easy.

<b>Serverless Architecture:</b> I designed and built distributed serverless applications with AWS CDK, SST, Serverless framework, and Terraform. There were fewer things to look after. It was easier to meet security requirements. And we went faster and could change quickly.
